%A2 · How Spanish works, part 2|The past, pronouns, comparing, right now, commands, por and para
@u53|El pasado: pretérito|What happened: the preterite|For finished actions in the past, Spanish uses the preterite. Regular endings: <b>hablé</b> (I spoke), <b>comí</b> (I ate), <b>viví</b> (I lived). The most common irregulars are <b>fui</b> (I went / I was), <b>tuve</b> (I had) and <b>hice</b> (I did).|Tell a friend five things you did last weekend.
#u53a|Regular verbs in the past
Hablé con mi jefe.|I spoke with my boss.
Compraste pan ayer.|You bought bread yesterday.
Mi hermana cocinó arroz.|My sister cooked rice.
Llegamos tarde a la fiesta.|We arrived late to the party.
Ellos trabajaron todo el día.|They worked all day.
Comí tacos en el mercado.|I ate tacos at the market.
¿Bebiste suficiente agua?|Did you drink enough water?
Mi abuelo vivió en Puebla.|My grandfather lived in Puebla.
Escribimos una carta.|We wrote a letter.
Abrieron una tienda nueva.|They opened a new shop.
#u53b|Irregular verbs in the past
Fui al doctor el lunes.|I went to the doctor on Monday.
Fuimos a la playa en abril.|We went to the beach in April.
Tuve un problema con el carro.|I had a problem with the car.
Hice la tarea anoche.|I did the homework last night.
Estuve en casa todo el fin de semana.|I was at home all weekend.
No pude dormir.|I couldn't sleep.
Ella dijo que sí.|She said yes.
Vinieron mis primos.|My cousins came.
¿Dónde pusiste las llaves?|Where did you put the keys?
Quise llamarte, pero no tuve tiempo.|I wanted to call you, but I didn't have time.
@u54|Lo, la, le: palabras que reemplazan|It, him, her, them: object pronouns|Short words stand for things and people you already mentioned, and go before the verb: <b>Lo tengo</b> (I have it), <b>La perdí</b> (I lost it), <b>Te llamo</b> (I'll call you).|Talk about a present you bought someone: what it was, who you gave it to, and what they said.
#u54a|It and them
¿El libro? Lo tengo yo.|The book? I have it.
¿La llave? La perdí.|The key? I lost it.
¿Los boletos? Los compré en línea.|The tickets? I bought them online.
¿Las tortillas? Las traigo yo.|The tortillas? I'll bring them.
Te llamo mañana.|I'll call you tomorrow.
¿Me ayudas, por favor?|Can you help me, please?
Nos invitaron a cenar.|They invited us to dinner.
#u54b|To him, to her, to them
Le doy el regalo a mi mamá.|I give the present to my mum.
Le escribí a Carlos.|I wrote to Carlos.
Les dije la verdad.|I told them the truth.
¿Le puedo hacer una pregunta?|May I ask you a question? (polite)
Mi abuela me contó una historia.|My grandmother told me a story.
Te mando la dirección.|I'll send you the address.
@u55|Comparar|Comparing|Compare with <b>más … que</b> (more … than) and <b>menos … que</b> (less … than); say "as … as" with <b>tan … como</b>. Better, worse, older and younger have words of their own, in the second part.|Compare two cities you know: size, weather, food and prices.
#u55a|More, less, as … as
Monterrey es más grande que Puebla.|Monterrey is bigger than Puebla.
El metro es más rápido que el camión.|The metro is faster than the bus.
Este hotel es menos caro que el otro.|This hotel is less expensive than the other one.
Mi hermano es tan alto como mi papá.|My brother is as tall as my dad.
Hoy no hace tanto calor como ayer.|Today it isn't as hot as yesterday.
Tengo más trabajo que tú.|I have more work than you.
#u55b|Better, worse, the most
Esta salsa es mejor que la otra.|This salsa is better than the other one.
El tráfico de hoy es peor que el de ayer.|Today's traffic is worse than yesterday's.
Mi hermana es mayor que yo.|My sister is older than me.
Soy el menor de la familia.|I am the youngest in the family.
Es la ciudad más bonita de México.|It is the most beautiful city in Mexico.
Este es el restaurante más caro del centro.|This is the most expensive restaurant in the centre.
@u56|Ahorita y órdenes|Right now, and telling people what to do|For what is happening now: <b>estar</b> + the -ando / -iendo form, as in <b>Estoy trabajando</b> (I'm working) or <b>¿Qué estás haciendo?</b> (What are you doing?). The second part shows how to tell people what to do, to a friend and politely.|Phone a friend and say what everyone at home is doing right now.
#u56a|What is happening right now
Estoy trabajando.|I'm working right now.
¿Qué estás haciendo?|What are you doing?
Los niños están jugando en el patio.|The children are playing in the yard.
Estamos esperando el camión.|We are waiting for the bus.
Mi papá está durmiendo.|My dad is sleeping.
Está lloviendo muy fuerte.|It's raining really hard.
Ahorita estoy manejando, te marco al rato.|I'm driving right now, I'll call you in a bit.
#u56b|Telling people what to do
Ven aquí.|Come here.
Espérame un momento.|Wait for me a moment.
Dime la verdad.|Tell me the truth.
Ponlo en la mesa.|Put it on the table.
Hazme un favor.|Do me a favour.
Venga mañana a las diez.|Come tomorrow at ten. (polite)
Espere aquí, por favor.|Wait here, please. (polite)
Llame a este número.|Call this number. (polite)
@u57|Por, para y cuándo|Por, para and how often|<b>Para</b> points to a goal or a deadline (<b>para el viernes</b>, by Friday); <b>por</b> to a reason, a route or a part of the day (<b>por la tarde</b>, in the afternoon). The second part is about how often you do things.|Describe your week: what you do every day, once a week and almost never.
#u57a|Por and para
Este regalo es para ti.|This present is for you.
Necesito el informe para el viernes.|I need the report by Friday.
Estudio español para viajar.|I study Spanish in order to travel.
Gracias por todo.|Thanks for everything.
Pasamos por el centro.|We went through the centre.
Te llamo por la tarde.|I'll call you in the afternoon.
Lo compré por cien pesos.|I bought it for a hundred pesos.
#u57b|How often
Voy al gimnasio dos veces a la semana.|I go to the gym twice a week.
Tomo café tres veces al día.|I drink coffee three times a day.
Casi nunca veo la tele.|I almost never watch TV.
Casi siempre llego a tiempo.|I'm almost always on time.
Visito a mis abuelos una vez al mes.|I visit my grandparents once a month.
De vez en cuando como en la calle.|Every now and then I eat out.
Todos los domingos comemos en familia.|Every Sunday we eat together as a family.
