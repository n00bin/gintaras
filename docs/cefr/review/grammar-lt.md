# Lithuanian grammar review book

Six worked-example lessons with practice keys. These are learning exercises, not an independent proficiency assessment.

## A1 · Place and agreement

Learn complete patterns for where someone lives and for describing a home. Lithuanian city and adjective forms change with their role in the sentence.

### Worked examples

- Kur gyveni? Aš gyvenu Vilniuje.
  - Where do you live? I live in Vilnius.
  - Vilniuje is the city form in this learned location pattern.
- Mano butas mažas.
  - My flat is small.
  - Butas and mažas are singular masculine forms.
- Mano namai maži.
  - My home is small.
  - Namai has a plural form, with the matching adjective maži.

### Practice key

1. Choose the city form for “Aš gyvenu …”.
   - Vilniuje **[key]**
   - Vilnius
   - Vilnių
   - Feedback: Use the learned location form Vilniuje.
2. Complete “Mano butas …”.
   - maži
   - mažas **[key]**
   - maža
   - Feedback: The adjective agrees with singular masculine butas.
3. Complete “Mano namai …”.
   - mažas
   - maža
   - maži **[key]**
   - Feedback: The plural form namai takes maži here.
4. Which question asks where someone lives?
   - Koks tavo vardas?
   - Kur gyveni? **[key]**
   - Ar nori arbatos?
   - Feedback: Kur asks where; gyveni means you live.
5. Use the example to write “I live in Vilnius”.
   - Learned response: Aš gyvenu Vilniuje.
   - Feedback: Keep the learned first-person verb and city form.
6. Use the example to write “My home is small”.
   - Learned response: Mano namai maži.
   - Feedback: Use the matching plural forms namai and maži.

## A2 · Time, reasons and conditions

Keep past events, future plans and conditions distinct. Use a reason to explain a changed plan.

### Worked examples

- Vakar dirbau. Rytoj dirbsiu.
  - Yesterday I worked. Tomorrow I will work.
  - Dirbau describes the past here; dirbsiu describes the future.
- Negaliu ateiti, nes turiu dirbti. Gal galime susitikti penktadienį?
  - I cannot come because I have to work. Perhaps we can meet on Friday?
  - Nes gives a reason; the second sentence proposes a replacement.
- Jei lis, susitikime kavinėje.
  - If it rains, let us meet in a café.
  - Jei introduces a condition, not a report that rain has already happened.

### Practice key

1. Complete the future plan: “Rytoj …”.
   - dirbau
   - dirbsiu **[key]**
   - dirbo
   - Feedback: Rytoj refers to tomorrow; dirbsiu is the learned future form.
2. Choose the connector giving the reason for not coming.
   - nes **[key]**
   - bet
   - arba
   - Feedback: Nes introduces the reason.
3. What does “Jei lis” express?
   - It rained yesterday
   - It is certainly raining now
   - A condition: if it rains **[key]**
   - Feedback: The clause describes a condition.
4. Which sentence proposes a replacement?
   - Vakar dirbau.
   - Gal galime susitikti penktadienį? **[key]**
   - Negaliu ateiti.
   - Feedback: The question suggests meeting on Friday.
5. Explain that you cannot come because you have to work.
   - Learned response: Negaliu ateiti, nes turiu dirbti.
   - Feedback: Connect the problem and reason with nes.
6. Write the learned café plan if it rains.
   - Learned response: Jei lis, susitikime kavinėje.
   - Feedback: Use the condition first, then the proposal.

## B1 · Connect events and explanations

A connected account distinguishes events, a contrast and an attributed explanation. Do not present every sentence as the same kind of claim.

### Worked examples

- Iš pradžių nusipirkau bilietą. Tačiau autobusas vėlavo.
  - At first I bought a ticket. However, the bus was delayed.
  - The first sentence reports an action; tačiau introduces a complication.
- Atvykau vėliau, bet spėjau į susitikimą.
  - I arrived later, but made it to the meeting.
  - Bet contrasts the late arrival with the successful outcome.
- Darbuotoja paaiškino, kad galime važiuoti kitu autobusu. Manau, kad svarbu turėti kitą planą.
  - The employee explained that we could take another bus. I think it is important to have another plan.
  - The first clause attributes information; manau marks the speaker’s view.

### Practice key

1. Choose the learned past action.
   - nusipirksiu
   - nusiperku
   - nusipirkau **[key]**
   - Feedback: Nusipirkau reports the completed purchase in the example.
2. Which connector introduces the complication after the ticket purchase?
   - Todėl
   - Tačiau **[key]**
   - Nes
   - Feedback: Tačiau introduces the contrast with the plan.
3. Who explains the alternative bus in the example?
   - The employee **[key]**
   - The writer’s friend
   - No source is identified
   - Feedback: Darbuotoja paaiškino identifies the source.
4. Which phrase explicitly introduces the speaker’s view?
   - Darbuotoja paaiškino
   - Autobusas vėlavo
   - Manau, kad **[key]**
   - Feedback: Manau means I think.
5. Write the example’s contrast between arriving late and making the meeting.
   - Learned response: Atvykau vėliau, bet spėjau į susitikimą.
   - Feedback: The outcome is contrasted with the late arrival.
6. Attribute the alternative bus information to the employee.
   - Learned response: Darbuotoja paaiškino, kad galime važiuoti kitu autobusu.
   - Feedback: The reporting clause identifies whose explanation this is.

## B2 · Concede, qualify and recommend

A concession can accept an objection without making it decisive. Conditional recommendation forms and limited claims let you express a position precisely.

### Worked examples

- Nors šis prieštaravimas pagrįstas, jis nėra lemiamas.
  - Although this objection is reasonable, it is not decisive.
  - Nors introduces an acknowledged concession.
- Todėl siūlyčiau lankstesnį modelį.
  - I would therefore suggest a more flexible model.
  - Siūlyčiau makes a conditional recommendation, rather than reporting a past suggestion.
- Toks sprendimas tiktų ne visiems.
  - Such a solution would not suit everyone.
  - Ne visiems does not mean that it would suit nobody.

### Practice key

1. Introduce the acknowledged concession.
   - Nors **[key]**
   - Nes
   - Todėl
   - Feedback: Nors means although in this pattern.
2. Which form expresses “I would suggest”?
   - siūliau
   - siūlyčiau **[key]**
   - siūlysiu
   - Feedback: Siūlyčiau is the conditional recommendation form.
3. What is warranted by “tiktų ne visiems”?
   - It suits nobody
   - It suits everyone
   - It would not suit everyone **[key]**
   - Feedback: The scope is limited: not everyone.
4. Does acknowledging a reasonable objection necessarily abandon the recommendation?
   - Yes, every concession reverses the position
   - No, the example distinguishes reasonable from decisive **[key]**
   - Yes, if nors is used
   - Feedback: A concession can qualify a position without reversing it.
5. Use the learned form to recommend a more flexible model.
   - Learned response: Todėl siūlyčiau lankstesnį modelį.
   - Feedback: Use siūlyčiau for the recommendation.
6. Acknowledge the objection without treating it as decisive.
   - Learned response: Nors šis prieštaravimas pagrįstas, jis nėra lemiamas.
   - Feedback: Preserve both the acknowledgement and its limit.

## C1 · Attribute and limit a conclusion

Reporting a source, interpreting evidence and recommending action are different functions. Preserve the qualification when reformulating.

### Worked examples

- Ataskaitoje teigiama, kad registracija tapo greitesnė.
  - The report states that registration became faster.
  - This attributes the claim to a report.
- Tai savaime neįrodo, kad sistema tinka visiems.
  - This does not in itself prove that the system suits everyone.
  - Savaime limits what the observation establishes; it does not deny every possible benefit.
- Sprendimą vertėtų peržiūrėti gavus papildomos informacijos.
  - The decision should be reconsidered when additional information is available.
  - The recommendation names a condition for reconsideration.

### Practice key

1. Which wording explicitly attributes a claim?
   - Sistema neabejotinai tobula.
   - Ataskaitoje teigiama, kad **[key]**
   - Visi privalo sutikti.
   - Feedback: The reporting construction identifies the source.
2. What does “savaime neįrodo” limit?
   - What follows from that observation alone **[key]**
   - Whether any observation exists
   - Whether any benefit is possible
   - Feedback: It limits the inference rather than denying everything.
3. Which reformulation preserves the second example?
   - The system benefits nobody
   - The system certainly suits everyone
   - This observation alone does not establish suitability for all **[key]**
   - Feedback: Preserve the evidential limit.
4. What triggers reconsideration in the third example?
   - A predetermined conclusion
   - Additional information **[key]**
   - The absence of every possible question
   - Feedback: Gavus papildomos informacijos names the condition.
5. State the learned limit on the general conclusion.
   - Learned response: Tai savaime neįrodo, kad sistema tinka visiems.
   - Feedback: Do not drop savaime or the scope visiems.
6. Give the conditional reconsideration recommendation.
   - Learned response: Sprendimą vertėtų peržiūrėti gavus papildomos informacijos.
   - Feedback: Preserve the condition for review.

## C2 · Negation, entailment and revision conditions

Fine differences in the scope of negation and conditional language can change an argument. Reformulate without strengthening what follows.

### Worked examples

- Ne visi dalyviai sutiko. Nė vienas dalyvis nesutiko.
  - Not all participants agreed. Not a single participant agreed.
  - The first sentence does not entail the second.
- Iš to neseka, kad bet koks pokytis būtų žalingas.
  - It does not follow that any change would be harmful.
  - Rejecting that inference does not establish that every change is safe.
- Jeigu atsirastų naujų duomenų, reikėtų peržiūrėti rekomendaciją.
  - If new evidence emerged, the recommendation would need review.
  - The hypothetical condition supplies a genuine revision trigger.

### Practice key

1. Which conclusion follows from “Ne visi dalyviai sutiko”?
   - Nobody agreed
   - Everyone agreed
   - Not everyone agreed **[key]**
   - Feedback: Do not widen not all into none.
2. Which sentence expresses the stronger negative claim?
   - Ne visi dalyviai sutiko.
   - Nė vienas dalyvis nesutiko. **[key]**
   - Both necessarily mean the same thing.
   - Feedback: Nė vienas excludes every participant.
3. Does rejecting an inference of harm prove universal safety?
   - No **[key]**
   - Yes
   - Only if the sentence is short
   - Feedback: Failure of one inference does not prove the opposite universal.
4. What does the final example commit the speaker to?
   - Never revising the recommendation
   - Ignoring new evidence
   - Revisiting it if the named condition occurs **[key]**
   - Feedback: The condition specifies when the recommendation must be reconsidered.
5. Reject the overbroad harmful-change inference using the example.
   - Learned response: Iš to neseka, kad bet koks pokytis būtų žalingas.
   - Feedback: Keep the inferential and quantifier limits.
6. Write the example’s hypothetical revision condition.
   - Learned response: Jeigu atsirastų naujų duomenų, reikėtų peržiūrėti rekomendaciją.
   - Feedback: Preserve both the condition and the required consequence.
