# Mexican Spanish grammar review book

Six worked-example lessons with practice keys. These are learning exercises, not an independent proficiency assessment.

## A1 · Origin, location and agreement

Learn origin and location as complete patterns. Use ser for the quality in these examples, estar for location, and match adjectives to their nouns.

### Worked examples

- ¿Dónde vives? Vivo en México. Soy de México.
  - Where do you live? I live in Mexico. I am from Mexico.
  - Vivo en gives a place of residence; soy de gives origin.
- La casa es pequeña. La cocina está aquí.
  - The house is small. The kitchen is here.
  - Es describes the quality here; está locates the kitchen.
- Los cuartos son pequeños.
  - The rooms are small.
  - The plural noun takes plural son and pequeños.

### Practice key

1. Say that you are from Mexico.
   - Soy de México. **[key]**
   - Estoy de México.
   - Vivo de México.
   - Feedback: The learned origin pattern is soy de.
2. Complete the location: “La cocina … aquí”.
   - es
   - está **[key]**
   - son
   - Feedback: Use está for location here.
3. Complete “Los cuartos son …”.
   - pequeña
   - pequeño
   - pequeños **[key]**
   - Feedback: The adjective agrees with the plural masculine noun.
4. Which question asks where someone lives?
   - ¿Cómo te llamas?
   - ¿Dónde vives? **[key]**
   - ¿Quieres un té?
   - Feedback: Dónde asks where; vives means you live.
5. Use the example to write “I live in Mexico”.
   - Learned response: Vivo en México.
   - Feedback: Use vivo en for residence.
6. Use the example to locate the kitchen here.
   - Learned response: La cocina está aquí.
   - Feedback: Use está for the location.

## A2 · Time, reasons and conditions

Distinguish completed past events from plans and conditional alternatives. Give a reason when changing an arrangement.

### Worked examples

- Ayer trabajé. Mañana voy a trabajar.
  - Yesterday I worked. Tomorrow I am going to work.
  - Trabajé reports the past event; voy a trabajar gives the plan.
- No puedo ir porque tengo que trabajar. ¿Podemos vernos el viernes?
  - I cannot go because I have to work. Can we meet on Friday?
  - Porque gives the reason; the question proposes another day.
- Si llueve, nos vemos en una cafetería.
  - If it rains, we will meet in a café.
  - Si introduces a condition, not a statement that it is already raining.

### Practice key

1. Choose the plan for tomorrow.
   - Ayer trabajé.
   - Mañana voy a trabajar. **[key]**
   - Trabajé ayer.
   - Feedback: Voy a trabajar expresses the planned future action.
2. Choose the connector giving the reason.
   - porque **[key]**
   - pero
   - o
   - Feedback: Porque introduces because.
3. What does “Si llueve” express?
   - It rained yesterday
   - It is certainly raining now
   - A condition: if it rains **[key]**
   - Feedback: The clause supplies a condition.
4. Which sentence proposes another day?
   - Ayer trabajé.
   - ¿Podemos vernos el viernes? **[key]**
   - No puedo ir.
   - Feedback: The question proposes Friday.
5. Explain that you cannot go because you have to work.
   - Learned response: No puedo ir porque tengo que trabajar.
   - Feedback: Connect the problem and reason with porque.
6. Write the learned café plan if it rains.
   - Learned response: Si llueve, nos vemos en una cafetería.
   - Feedback: Preserve the condition and the plan.

## B1 · Narrate and attribute

Use the taught past forms to distinguish an event from its background. Connect a complication and outcome, and identify the source of an explanation.

### Worked examples

- Compré un boleto, pero el autobús llevaba retraso.
  - I bought a ticket, but the bus was running late.
  - Compré gives the event; llevaba describes the background situation here.
- Llegué más tarde, pero pude asistir a la reunión.
  - I arrived later, but was able to attend the meeting.
  - Pero introduces the contrast with the successful outcome.
- La empleada explicó que había otra ruta. Creo que conviene tener otro plan.
  - The employee explained that there was another route. I think it is useful to have another plan.
  - The reporting clause identifies the source; creo marks the speaker’s view.

### Practice key

1. Choose the completed purchase in the example.
   - Compraré
   - Compro
   - Compré **[key]**
   - Feedback: Compré reports the completed past purchase.
2. Which form describes the ongoing delay in the example?
   - compré
   - llevaba **[key]**
   - asistir
   - Feedback: Llevaba supplies the background situation.
3. Who explains the other route?
   - The employee **[key]**
   - The writer’s friend
   - No source is identified
   - Feedback: La empleada explicó names the source.
4. Which wording explicitly introduces the speaker’s view?
   - La empleada explicó
   - El autobús llevaba retraso
   - Creo que **[key]**
   - Feedback: Creo que means I think that.
5. Write the learned contrast between late arrival and attending the meeting.
   - Learned response: Llegué más tarde, pero pude asistir a la reunión.
   - Feedback: Preserve the contrast and successful outcome.
6. Attribute the other route information to the employee.
   - Learned response: La empleada explicó que había otra ruta.
   - Feedback: Keep the reporting clause and its source.

## B2 · Concession and conditional recommendation

Acknowledge an objection without making it decisive. Conditional recommendation forms and the scope of a negative claim matter.

### Worked examples

- Aunque la objeción es razonable, no resulta decisiva.
  - Although the objection is reasonable, it is not decisive.
  - Aunque introduces the acknowledged concession.
- Por eso propondría un modelo más flexible.
  - I would therefore propose a more flexible model.
  - Propondría makes a conditional recommendation, rather than reporting a past proposal.
- La solución no funcionaría para todo el mundo.
  - The solution would not work for everyone.
  - Not everyone does not mean nobody.

### Practice key

1. Introduce the acknowledged concession.
   - Aunque **[key]**
   - Porque
   - Por eso
   - Feedback: Aunque introduces although here.
2. Choose “I would propose”.
   - proponía
   - propondría **[key]**
   - propondré
   - Feedback: Propondría is the conditional form.
3. What does the third example establish?
   - It would work for nobody
   - It would work for everyone
   - It would not work for everyone **[key]**
   - Feedback: Preserve the scope of the limited negative claim.
4. Does a reasonable objection necessarily defeat the recommendation?
   - Yes, every concession reverses the position
   - No, the example distinguishes reasonable from decisive **[key]**
   - Yes, whenever aunque appears
   - Feedback: Acknowledgement need not be abandonment.
5. Use the example to recommend a more flexible model.
   - Learned response: Por eso propondría un modelo más flexible.
   - Feedback: Use the conditional recommendation form.
6. Acknowledge the objection while retaining its limit.
   - Learned response: Aunque la objeción es razonable, no resulta decisiva.
   - Feedback: Preserve both clauses.

## C1 · Attribution and evidential limits

Separate what a report states from what the evidence establishes and what you recommend. Do not drop the condition in a reformulation.

### Worked examples

- El informe señala que el registro se volvió más rápido.
  - The report states that registration became faster.
  - The reporting phrase attributes the claim.
- Esto no demuestra por sí solo que el sistema sirva para todos.
  - This does not in itself prove that the system suits everyone.
  - Por sí solo limits the inference without denying every benefit.
- La decisión debería revisarse cuando se disponga de más información.
  - The decision should be reconsidered when more information becomes available.
  - The recommendation preserves a condition for reconsideration.

### Practice key

1. Which phrase explicitly attributes a claim?
   - El sistema es indudablemente perfecto.
   - El informe señala que **[key]**
   - Todos deben estar de acuerdo.
   - Feedback: The reporting construction identifies the source.
2. What does “por sí solo” limit?
   - What the observation establishes on its own **[key]**
   - Whether any observation exists
   - Whether a benefit is possible at all
   - Feedback: It bounds the inference.
3. Which reformulation preserves the second example?
   - Nobody can benefit
   - The system certainly suits everyone
   - This observation alone does not prove suitability for all **[key]**
   - Feedback: Keep the evidential limit.
4. What condition is named for review?
   - A predetermined conclusion
   - Further information becoming available **[key]**
   - The end of all possible discussion
   - Feedback: Cuando se disponga de más información identifies the trigger.
5. State the taught limit on the general conclusion.
   - Learned response: Esto no demuestra por sí solo que el sistema sirva para todos.
   - Feedback: Keep por sí solo and para todos.
6. Write the conditional reconsideration recommendation.
   - Learned response: La decisión debería revisarse cuando se disponga de más información.
   - Feedback: Preserve the trigger for review.

## C2 · Negation, entailment and revision conditions

Small differences in negation scope and hypothetical conditions can substantially change an argument. Preserve what follows, and what does not.

### Worked examples

- No todos los participantes estuvieron de acuerdo. Ningún participante estuvo de acuerdo.
  - Not all participants agreed. Not a single participant agreed.
  - The first does not entail the second.
- De ahí no se sigue que cualquier cambio sea perjudicial.
  - It does not follow that any change would be harmful.
  - Rejecting this inference does not establish that every change is safe.
- Si aparecieran nuevos datos, habría que revisar la recomendación.
  - If new evidence emerged, the recommendation would need review.
  - The hypothetical condition provides a genuine revision trigger.

### Practice key

1. What follows from “No todos los participantes estuvieron de acuerdo”?
   - Nobody agreed
   - Everyone agreed
   - Not everyone agreed **[key]**
   - Feedback: Do not strengthen not all into none.
2. Which sentence makes the stronger negative claim?
   - No todos los participantes estuvieron de acuerdo.
   - Ningún participante estuvo de acuerdo. **[key]**
   - Both necessarily mean the same thing.
   - Feedback: Ningún excludes every participant.
3. Does rejecting the harmful-change inference establish universal safety?
   - No **[key]**
   - Yes
   - Only if the sentence is short
   - Feedback: Failure of an inference does not prove the opposite universal.
4. What commitment is made in the third example?
   - Never revise the recommendation
   - Ignore new evidence
   - Reconsider it if the named condition occurs **[key]**
   - Feedback: The condition names a real trigger for revision.
5. Reject the overbroad harmful-change inference using the example.
   - Learned response: De ahí no se sigue que cualquier cambio sea perjudicial.
   - Feedback: Keep the inferential limit and quantifier scope.
6. Write the taught hypothetical revision condition.
   - Learned response: Si aparecieran nuevos datos, habría que revisar la recomendación.
   - Feedback: Preserve both condition and consequence.
